Evidence

What we measured, and what we did not.

Every number on the site comes from this page's data. It is a small experiment and it says so.

Measured

  • The YouCam scanner returns identical scores for an identical file: 0 points of difference over three scans.
  • Changing only the light moves scores: up to 11 points on one concern, up to 3 years of skin age.
  • Matching the light to the baseline cut the total gap from 84 to 33 points across the 12 cases below.

Assumed

  • That the light can be read from the middle of the frame (a fixed crop), as a mean and spread per colour channel.
  • That a score change smaller than the noise band for its concern is not evidence of anything.
  • That the bands measured on a programmatic light change carry over to a real bulb.

Not validated

  • Anything on real camera photos, makeup, shadows, a changed pose or a different phone.
  • Whether a score change is a real change in skin: Same Light says a change beats the light noise, not that skincare worked.
  • More than 3 faces, and darker skin tones (the 3 faces include one; the skin-colour guard was looked at on four faces only).

How the light is read, and when Same Light refuses

  • The light is read from a fixed crop of the middle of the frame (the middle 50% by 60%). Hair, makeup, shadows and a changed pose can contaminate it.
  • If almost nothing in that crop looks like skin, no verdict is given and the photo is rejected as unreadable. If under a third looks like skin, the result carries a warning. The colour test is loose on purpose and is not applied to very dark crops, where it cannot tell skin from dark hair: a check on all ten Monk Skin Tone swatches found it refused the darkest tone before that fix, and none now. Flat swatches are not faces, so this is a diagnostic, not a fairness validation.
  • A light gap beyond the measured range (exposure ±27%, warmth ±0.08) is shown with how much the light explains and marked "can't certify". Beyond 35% / 0.25 it asks for a retake.
